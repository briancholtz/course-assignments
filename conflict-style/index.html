What each style means

Avoidance

Staying away from the disagreement — keeping it to yourself rather than opening it up.

Competition

Pressing your own side, using influence, authority or expertise to carry the decision.

Compromise

Splitting the difference — trading concessions to find a workable middle.

Accommodation

Going along with what the other wants, satisfying their needs ahead of your own.

Collaboration

Working the problem together, pooling information to find something that satisfies both.

Ranges

The same five ranges apply to each style, for each person.

21–25Very strong style
16–20Strong style
11–15Average style
6–10Weak style
5Very weak style

The textbook prints the bottom range as 0–5. Five items rated 1–5 cannot total below 5, so 5 is the only score it can contain.

By comparing your total scores for the different styles, you can discover which conflict style you rely on most heavily and which you use least. By comparing Person A with Person B, you can see how your style varies or stays the same across relationships.

Your scores are indicative of how you responded to a particular conflict at a specific time, and might change if you picked a different conflict or a different period. This is not a personality test that labels or categorizes you — it gives you a sense of your more dominant and less dominant conflict styles.
